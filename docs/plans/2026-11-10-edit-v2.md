# Edit V2: the agent writes the graphics as code

Started Nov 10 2026. The in-app agent writes each graphic and sound as code, the person watches it play the moment it's written and steers it in a few words, and what's made is kept in V2's cloud library for the channel. Built (mamagi and the library on prod since Nov 14; Edit's side reaches the team with the next nightly): effects as code played live, their renders, transparency, the agent's looks (every_frame, beside, zoom), sounds as code, the library with versions, teaching (`agent.teach`), channels and groups. V2 is channels only (Bodycam HQ and niches went Nov 14). How it works is in app/AGENTS.md.

What holds (Sphe): playback is instant and comes first; code over tool calls wherever code makes sense; V2 is for graphics (pictures, footage and voices come from outside); the team picks the model (Fable for new effects, Opus for editing and reusing them), nothing routes it.

## What V1 can't do, and how much more V2 does

Sphe, Nov 11: what a team member using V1 can't do "cause we freeze effects", and "a quantifiable number like 3x". Kept up to date as V2 changes: move a row when something is built, and recount.

**Why V1 can't:** every graphic in V1 is a frozen house effect (the evidence board, the File Viewer, the map, the charges screen, the callout, the waveform…), each one look with the knobs we gave it. A team member gets exactly those; anything else, even a different take on one of them, waited on us to change Edit and ship a nightly. In V2 the agent writes the effect, so the ask itself is enough.

**The number: 4x.** Of 40 motion-graphics capabilities After Effects is used for (table below), V1 does 7 fully and V2 29. Scored yes 1, partly 0.5, no 0, it's 15.5 against 32.5 (about 2x the breadth). 27 of the 40 are things a V1 user can't have at all or only partly. Neither number counts that within the rows V1 does, it's one look each, where V2 makes a new one every time.

**What a team member can't do in V1** (V2 can):
- Animate type any way but the presets (fade, rise, pop, typewriter, words): per-letter motion, text on a path, 3D text.
- Draw their own shapes, lines, arrows, icons or illustrations, morph them, or mask with them.
- Particles, flares and light rays, real distortion, liquify and mesh warps.
- 3D: layers, cameras, lights, depth of field, simple 3D objects.
- Charts, infographics and data graphics.
- Graphics that move with the sound (beyond the house waveform).
- Stick something to a surface in the footage (four-corner tracking); steady a point.
- Glass, lenses and refraction over the footage; any blur but the round or rectangular house one.
- Footage in their own frames: split screens, video walls, picture in picture of any design.
- Their own transitions (shape wipes, morphs, whips of any kind) beyond the house set.
- Their own grain, textures, glows, shadows and blend looks.
- Change a house graphic's look. JJ's own asks that waited on us: the evidence board moving and zooming around rather than only downward, photos scattered at sizes and tilts (Nov 2, 10); intro captions with the speaker's label, a full black box and on the speaker's side (Nov 5-10); a 911 call as a red and blue glowing line crossfading between caller and dispatcher (Nov 2); four blurred boxes revealed one by one (FPC); the map's dot red with a glow (Nov 5); "*gunshot*" flashing on screen (OF).
