# Where the team would feel the difference

My first pick for nightly-88 is **resume interrupted Script uploads**. At JJ’s reported upstream speed, sending the preview in his latest report takes at least 44 minutes. The bigger change is to let someone else read the script during those 44 minutes.

I reviewed the Nov 18 reports from JJ (nightly-86, 17:19 UTC), Marc (84, 17:16 UTC) and Sphe (85, 07:11 UTC), targeted cloud logs, the media plan and measurements, the morning audit, and the last week of changes through `5d56e830`. Grouped log counts can include retries of one operation. Estimates below are engineering time including focused verification. “Fits 88” means an item could ship independently.

1. **Keep the bytes JJ has already sent.** JJ’s report caught a live Script upload at 469,762,048 of 2,638,396,684 bytes. His log at 11:48:23 also records a proxy upload failing on DNS. These are separate observations. In `writer/cloud.ts:399–423` the part receipts live in memory and a failure aborts the upload. The next push chooses a fresh object key. Use the resumable upload machinery in `cloud.ts`, persisting the destination, source identity and confirmed parts across attempts. At the observed progress, a disconnect could otherwise cost about eight minutes of sending again. **1 day · medium risk · fits 88** if a disconnect and process restart resume the same file and a changed or deleted source retires the old attempt.

2. **Let a teammate open the script while its video travels.** The same 2.64 GB file delays more than playback. `writer/cloud.ts:1230–1286` sends changed files before the body, and the sync loop awaits each script’s push before the next. A producer on another Mac can therefore wait for footage before seeing a new script or even an unrelated revision. Publish the readable text and its required small assets first. Let verified video attach later against its source version, with “Footage arriving” only where playback needs it. **3–5 days · high risk · after 88.** Exercise two Macs editing, deleting and replacing footage during transfer. Measure save-to-readable on the second Mac separately from save-to-playable.

3. **Finish backing up a saved export after its Mac restarts.** Marc’s report still lists an export archive as “cut short by 3 restarts in a row,” awaiting unstick. This is the cloud copy of an already-rendered file. `exports.ts` hands it to an archive job, and the archive wrapper ends that job in a finally block. Keep a recoverable upload record until the remote object is confirmed. Credit new confirmed parts against the existing restart limit and park genuine repeated failures. Marc could close his laptop and later retrieve the finished file on another Mac without asking support or rendering again. **1–2 days · medium risk · after 88.** Verify against a changed or removed local output and an already-completed remote object before enabling recovery for older jobs.

4. **Make a cloud sign-in problem actionable once.** Marc’s report says signed in, relay responding in 37 ms, scripts synced, yet one project reports “Sign in to Edit.” It groups ten renewal warnings mentioning 401 over two days. Whether that particular error was still live is unclear. The relay already retries a refused token once, and a successful normal renewal clears the entry’s failure (`relay.ts:299–327`, `cloud_projects.ts:1427–1445`). Reconcile the *affected project’s* last failure with a fresh authorized operation. If access recovers, clear its stale message. If the session really expired, offer one account action beside the affected work. Keep local edits and the existing lock check. **About 1 day · medium risk · fits 88 if reproduced.** Replay token refresh, actual sign-out and lost ownership separately. A healthy relay ping alone says nothing about write access.

5. **Keep ordinary clicks fast as the Script library grows.** JJ has 121 scripts. On its three-second loop, `writer/cloud.ts:808–835` reads and parses eligible scripts, and `localFiles` recursively lists directories and stats files synchronously before unchanged entries are skipped. That shares the Bun thread serving the window. Mark script IDs dirty at local write and asset-completion points. Reconcile older or externally changed folders incrementally in the background. The expected gain is less intermittent lag opening a script or saving a change. The per-round stall on JJ’s disk is unmeasured. **2–3 days · medium risk · after 88.** First replay his library size, measure longest event-loop pause and foreground request latency, and check that an asset arriving without a text edit still syncs.

6. **Serve an already-reviewed motion sample during export.** `motion_review.ts:17–32` takes the sole review slot and refuses the call during an export *before* looking for the saved observations. Last week’s `09f64658` improved which observations are reusable. Let exact cache hits return before the slot and export checks, and recheck on admission for misses. The agent can answer a question about work it already checked while another project exports. The morning sample had 57 timed reviews with a 31 s median. That is the cost of a typical timed call, not the measured saving on a hit. **2–4 hours · low risk · fits 88.** Verify a matching hit launches no engine or model work and a changed clip still misses.

7. **Give the agent the context it needs when its edit is rejected.** Sphe’s report groups 24 stale-plan errors, with one example expecting revision 1 when the edit was at 1093. The write guard correctly rejected it in 17 ms. The missing cost is the trip back through the model to request fresh context. When the base snapshot is unavailable, return the current revision and a compact read of the affected span with the rejection (`edit_tools.ts:425–441`). The agent can revise its plan immediately. Keep the write guard and require a fresh plan. **About 1 day · medium risk · fits 88 for operations with a known span.** Measure turns that actually repeat a context read and their time to a successful write. The grouped errors do not establish 24 user-visible stalls.

8. **Make a reopened import tell the truth about elapsed time.** In `writer/import-screen.tsx` “Running for” begins when this component first observes a step. A person returning from Home can see a long job apparently start its clock again. The same detail also appears above the meter and under the active step. Use the job’s recorded start across reopen and restart, suppress elapsed time when its start is unknown, and show the current detail once. JJ can check a case, leave, and come back to a consistent account of its progress. **2–4 hours · low risk · fits 88.** Check that a genuine new attempt gets the intended start time.

9. **Give a failed export one obvious next action.** In `ShareDialog.tsx:341–361` and its footer, an ended attempt can show both Retry and the primary Export button. Retry targets the previous attempt while Export follows the current controls. Give the unchanged failed attempt one primary action carrying its destination, such as “Retry upload.” Changing destination or scope should switch the primary action to the newly requested export. After a deliberate cancellation, offer “Export again.” **3–5 hours · low to medium risk · fits 88.** Check a failed review upload with a good local file so the action actually reuses the render, and test marked ranges separately.

10. **Open the current script Doc in one click.** The Docs dialog knows whether the export revision matches the script. Even when it matches, the primary action is “Export again,” which makes a fresh Doc, while the link to the existing Doc is secondary (`writer/docs-dialog.tsx:56–60,130–180`). Make “Open current Doc” primary for a matching revision and “Export updated Doc” primary after the script changes. Keep deliberate duplicate and draft creation under secondary actions. Reviewers keep commenting on the same copy when the producer simply wants to reopen it. **2–4 hours · low risk · fits 88.** Preserve access to fresh export if someone deleted the file in Drive. Count duplicate same-revision exports before estimating savings.

11. **Show the comments when the producer opens the Doc’s comments section.** The dialog clears comments on open and requires a separate read button before it can offer the unresolved comments for a rewrite (`writer/docs-dialog.tsx:74–80,94–101,215–259`). Load once on opening that section for an authorized person, show unanswered comments first, and retain refresh for a Doc being reviewed live. This removes a round of “is there feedback yet?” while keeping the rewrite itself deliberate. **2–4 hours · low risk · fits 88.** Verify a late response cannot populate a different script’s panel.

## What to remove from the screen

Remove the repeated import detail and its unreliable clock first. In the export footer, remove the competing primary action after failure. In the Google Doc dialog, drop “as in Repack” and the storage-account explanation, retaining who can open the link. Put “Export draft” and same-revision duplication one level down. Those are concrete changes to existing controls and leave the producer’s meaningful choices accessible.

## What to measure first

Use a two-Mac handoff of a script with a 2.6 GB preview at 8 Mbit/s. Record when text opens on the second Mac, when playback starts, and confirmed bytes resent after a disconnect near completion. Separately time one full idle sync round with 120 populated script folders while opening a project. Those two tests distinguish uplink time, queue delay and local filesystem stalls. On Marc’s case, pair the next successful authenticated project operation with the exact time the 401 disappears. For the UI changes, observe one producer reopening an unchanged Doc and recovering a failed share before assigning a time saving.
