## Findings ranked by impact

### 1. Disk exhaustion crosses from disposable video into project bookkeeping — critical

**Evidence.** All 309 M4 “no space left” lines fit three separate hours. Nov 16 22:00–22:59 had 58, Nov 17 22:00–22:59 had 121, and Nov 18 05:00–05:59 had 130. On Nov 18 at 05:09:21 multiple effects failed to create their frames directory. At 05:09:55 Writer could no longer write its sync ledger. Kenobi at Nov 17 00:02:43 failed concurrent whole-preview downloads and at 00:05:55 could no longer create thumbnail directories. The 955 MB, 53,244-frame piece 36 is documented in `app/apps/server/src/native_preview.ts:105`. The M4 logs independently confirm the three bursts. The exact bytes of that piece come from the fix's recorded measurement.

**Cause.** Nightly-85 now remuxes the local init segment and one fragment (`app/apps/server/src/native_preview.ts:112`, `app/apps/server/src/proxy_pieces.ts:656`). It still accepts an existing `native/<n>.mp4` solely by existence, and skips rebuilding it at lines 133, 170–188. An upgraded installation can keep playing and storing the bad output. Two concurrent remuxes can start without a storage claim. Export admission checks free bytes per device at `app/apps/server/src/exports.ts:1583`, whereas the other writers' claims are a process-wide sum with no device at `app/apps/server/src/storage.ts:87`. Cleanup already runs over budget or below its floor. Its 12-hour project protection narrows to 10 minutes under pressure, with additional file freshness and pin checks (`app/apps/server/src/cache_cleanup.ts:69`, 596, 629–657). The blanket “only cleans under 1 GB, never within 12 h” diagnosis misses both the existing protections and the gap.

**Fix.** Version the native derived-file layout. Reclaim obsolete native MP4s incrementally after readers release them. Retain source fragments, in-flight uploads and originals. Introduce a device-scoped admission operation in storage.ts that checks, reserves, adjusts remaining bytes and releases in finally. Start with native copies using the actual fragment byte size, effect chunks and joins, removal scratch, and export chunks/output. Give foreground writes priority and shed speculative work before reaching the working floor. If the target disk cannot be measured, defer speculative writes. Current stat failure returns infinity at storage.ts:68. Preserve streaming when a copy cannot fit.

**Effort and risk.** 3–5 h for versioning and native admission, 12–20 h for shared admission. Medium risk. Reclaiming the sole local copy of an upload or counting the wrong external volume would be worse than an unnecessary cache miss.

**Verify.** Upgrade a populated 84 fixture with an oversized late piece. Check its first and last frame, packet count, duration and cache footprint after reopening. Run two native cuts, removal and a share against a falling fake free-space reading, with output first on the cache volume and then on another volume. Assert the reserve holds, a metadata save succeeds, all claims release after failure, and open readers survive cleanup. The existing new-file packet test is at `app/apps/server/test/native_sources.test.ts:201`.

### 2. One removal result can invalidate the rest of the edit — high

**Evidence and cause.** Text and watermark completion each replace matching strip media with a fresh ID and save the project (`app/apps/server/src/text_removal.ts:1694`, `app/apps/server/src/watermark_removal.ts:525`). On a mismatch, `app/apps/server/src/clean_strips.ts:253` removes *every* overlay, drops empty lanes and rebuilds them with fresh clip IDs. `app/apps/server/src/effect_inputs.ts:177` correctly narrows to clips beneath the effect in its time span, but includes those clip objects and media IDs in the hash. An unchanged overlay inside a distant effect can acquire a new hash from a strip completed elsewhere. The project save also wakes removal again after two seconds (`app/apps/server/src/removal_schedule.ts:500`). The `56bbe79e` cross-Mac hash fix addressed paths. This identity change happens on the same Mac. M4 had 25 revision conflicts, including expected 1/current 1093 at Nov 18 05:40:43. That example demonstrates a stale plan, not proof that removal caused all conflicts. The reported 50 clips every 2–5 minutes was not independently countable from these logs.

**Fix.** Diff desired strips against existing strips by source, treatment, range, parent and placement. Retain the media, lane and clip IDs for unchanged entries. Batch simultaneous completions into one store transaction. Hash material picture dependencies, including layer order, transforms and content version. Avoid excluding all IDs blindly because relationships can affect pixels. Retain the stale-revision check on user edits. `app/apps/server/src/edit_tools.ts:320` already allows some background-only changes.

**Effort and risk.** 8–16 h. Medium-high risk around trim, parenting and overlapping strips. **Verify** with two disjoint effects, 50 overlays and one changed strip. Assert stable IDs and render keys outside the affected span, one new render within it, no save on identical input, and matching frames before/after for unaffected ranges. Also exercise reorder, overlap, trim, reopen and cloud backfill.

### 3. Vmake treats uncertain completion as permission to submit again — high

**Evidence.** On M4, `edit-10880a4b-3s.mp4` logged 32% at Nov 18 03:05:32 (406 s elapsed) and 04:59:06 (7,220 s). The available M4 INFO/WARN stream has 38 “still processing at 32%” lines for this card, 24 warnings across pieces mentioning the two-hour deadline and 21 “sent again” lines across failure reasons. These are not 24 lost jobs or 21 proven duplicate charges. Versions changed from six concurrent workers to three (`f6a7a820`, `152fd5fe`), and `714dc1e4` / `684f01b2` persisted more restart state. The relay already enforces three browser places at `app/apps/relay/src/vmake.ts:73`.

**Cause.** The browser looks for cards for eight seconds and treats a failed card evaluation like absence (`app/apps/server/src/vmake_web.ts:613`). A still-processing card gets a two-hour deadline and then a piece-scoped error (lines 681–728). `app/apps/server/src/text_removal.ts:1495` increments the outstanding attempt for piece errors and clears it after provider success *before* saving the result to the shelf. A crash in that interval loses the durable handoff. The name alone cannot distinguish delayed card discovery, a browser with a different history, a failed upload and a site-side terminal failure. The export readiness hook can wait three hours (`app/apps/server/src/removal_schedule.ts:131`).

**Fix.** Persist the account, source hash/range, mode, attempt, submission intent, known card or result reference, last provider progress and next check. After uncertain submission, poll/reconcile under the same identity with increasing intervals and show the stalled state. Release the browser only if the job can be reattached safely. Otherwise park that submission and expose its blocker. Permit a new charge only after a definitive failed/refunded result or explicit approval. Commit the verified result locally or on the shelf before clearing outstanding state. Keep the existing blur and separate actual visual rejection from a lost transport acknowledgement.

**Effort and risk.** 10–18 h with a provider ID, longer if only page history survives. High integration risk. **Verify** with a delayed card list, missing history in an extra browser, a 32% plateau, and an interrupted handoff immediately after download. Count provider submissions, completed strips, free browser places, export wait and spend. Require one submission for one ambiguous attempt across restart. A parking decision must be visible and recoverable.

### 4. Long effects monopolize the machine's only render place — high

**Evidence.** M4 completed a 34 min 18 s render on Nov 17 at 23:39:51 and a 33 min 26 s render on Nov 18 at 05:01:39. At 04:51:35 a different project's title-card export tool returned after 10 min with “waiting behind … render”. That is direct cross-project impact. I could not independently recover the exact queue depth of 30. Across 441 M4 “rendered (N frames in T s)” lines the median effective throughput was 12.2 fps, P95 26.5 fps. Effective throughput includes setup and non-capture work. The slowest 5% were at or below 0.3 fps.

**Cause.** `app/apps/server/src/effects.ts:1706` creates a single semaphore. Lines 1849–1858 hold it for the whole draw, including chunk waits for playback to idle (around line 2305). Chrome captures PNGs frame by frame at line 2247. The implementation already resumes completed chunks, overlaps encoding with capture and limits PNGs in flight. `4908719b` changed admission from reserving the maximum bitrate of the whole video to the in-flight estimate and actual join size (lines 2221, 2320, 2398). This is in 85. Its commit records the earlier space refusal. Logs confirm space refusals, without an independent measurement of exactly 30 GB.

**Fix.** Schedule at encoded chunk boundaries, with visible/export-blocking work first and round-robin service across projects. Drop superseded generations before dispatch, reuse completed chunks and retain aging for long jobs. Keep one active capture initially on an 8 GB Mac. Two Chrome processes could compound CPU, memory and disk pressure. Use standard compositor text for ordinary captions. `9a36fa53` already implements a large part of this gain for newly generated in-video subtitles.

**Effort and risk.** 10–18 h, medium. Opening Chrome again on every chunk can cost the throughput gained. **Verify** with an hour of captions in project A and a 5 s title in B. Request B mid-render. Measure time until B begins (target at most one active chunk plus dispatch), A's total wall time, resident bytes, playback frame drops, and output frame equality across resumes. Compare warm and cold runs.

### 5. Sleep and restart use different, incomplete definitions of work — high

**Evidence.** M4 at Nov 18 05:12:43 logged “nothing running: the Mac may sleep again”. Seven seconds later removal logged a 3 s piece still at 39%, and it was still polling at 05:16:05. This proves Edit released its assertion during removal. Device logs do not establish the instant macOS actually slept. At Nov 17 22:30:00 an update interrupted two approximately 22-minute agent turns, and at 22:30:16 both logged “carrying on”. Marc's latest report had an archive given up after three restarts and an import waiting with 9 of 18 files unavailable.

**Cause.** `app/apps/server/src/serve.ts:477` checks exports, archives, footage imports, Drive and agents. The polling holder at `app/apps/server/src/awake.ts` never sees an isolated effect or removal worker. `app/apps/server/src/busy.ts:24` always returns an empty list of work an update would lose. The separate ordinary remote restart checks `busyWith` then schedules the restart three seconds later (`app/apps/server/src/remote_actions.ts:473`). The updater does recheck its callback before preparing to exec (`app/apps/server/src/update.ts:213`). Turn recovery has a ten-minute horizon (`app/apps/server/src/agent.ts:1682`, `app/apps/server/src/jobs.ts:99`). Three separate definitions of “idle”, “can checkpoint” and “may sleep” give inconsistent answers.

**Fix.** Have active draw, encode, local removal and provider handoff register their work, sleep requirement and checkpoint status. Drive keep-awake and restart readiness from those records. Check readiness again immediately before an ordinary remote restart. Allow sleep during backoff when a durable provider job is safely parked. Preserve explicit forced restart semantics.

**Effort and risk.** 1 h for a minimal active-worker predicate if exposed, 5–9 h for restart coordination and checkpoint contracts. Medium battery and stuck-assertion risk. **Verify** isolated render/removal with no agent or export, hold-release on success, exceptions and cancel, and an ordinary remote restart requested immediately before a job begins. Compare saved submissions and completed chunks before/after an upgrade in a private fixture.

### 6. Export-ahead spends real disk on edits it already knows will change — high

**Evidence and cause.** M4 logged ten ahead starts, including Nov 18 01:59:52 on the long edit. The reported 7.2 GB chunk directory was not measured in the retrieved logs. `app/apps/server/src/export_ahead.ts:37` waits two minutes after a save. Lines 84–115 check battery, agents, smoothing, link use and other ahead work. They do not check outstanding removal or unfinished effects. Lines 140–159 cancel on changes and retain reusable chunks. `app/apps/server/src/exports.ts:842` checks capacity and estimated free space at start. It does not give ahead work a byte quota. `app/apps/server/src/cache_cleanup.ts:512` ages interrupted video and sound chunks out after three days. Cleaning old final ahead files does not cap today's intermediate bytes. Conversely, `app/apps/server/src/retry_after_update.ts:180` dequeues a failed export/share and at line 187 recursively clears its chunk folder even for failures unrelated to corrupted chunks.

**Fix.** Gate ahead admission on picture dependencies being settled. Let user-requested export retain priority and share intermediates. Track speculative bytes produced, joined and reused by project. Cap speculative cache based on disposable room on its actual volume. Preserve verified chunks after a network, auth or upload failure. Quarantine just the rejected chunk when integrity fails. Require a durable retry start before removing its queued marker.

**Effort and risk.** 1 h for the removal gate, 6–12 h for budgets and selective retry. Low risk for deferral, medium for cache ownership. **Verify** by completing a strip every three minutes on an hour-long edit. Speculative bytes should stay flat until settled. On a failed upload, a retry should read existing valid chunks and still produce an equivalent final export. Measure time to share on an unchanged edit to guard the value of ahead work.

### 7. Cache cleanup can stall the server while it is already under pressure — high

**Evidence and cause.** `app/apps/server/src/cache_cleanup.ts:208` recursively stats and lists the entire tree synchronously, deduplicating hard links per traversal. Lines 434, 596 and 632 calculate full-tree size at different phases. On-demand `makeRoom` at line 776 also sizes the tree before entering the cleaner. `app/apps/server/src/storage.ts:125` invokes that pass on the caller's thread with a five-minute cooldown. Previous fixes addressed other main-thread stalls one caller at a time, including repeated JSON parsing (nightlies 63 and 64) and macOS folder prompts (`0ab650df`, `249b732d`). Kenobi reported “Still waiting for frame 158 after 10 s” at Nov 17 12:38:41. Direct causation by this scan was not measured.

**Fix.** First count scans and their duration. Reuse a per-pass inventory and decrement reclaimed bytes, deduplicating by device and inode. Move inventory collection off the Bun request loop or walk in bounded batches. Revalidate file identity, active readers and pending uploads on the server immediately before deletion. Let concurrent pressure requests await one reclamation pass and recheck free space afterward.

**Effort and risk.** 6–12 h, medium. Stale inventories and hard links need explicit tests. **Verify** on a fixture with 100,000 blocks, hard links, files disappearing and an external cache. Record event-loop lag, health P95/P99 and wall time for a sweep with playback active. Require identical protected files and freed-byte totals.

### 8. Cloud outages generate independent retries and threaten lock renewal — high

**Evidence.** Across four Macs 2,732 distinct warnings/errors matched connection/timeout wording. JJ contributed 2,298. His reports had review polls waiting 120 s, writer offline and failed uploads in the same window. At Nov 17 17:15:41 his `edit:renewLock` failed after 15 s. Marc at Nov 17 18:50:58 logged Writer offline, a failed reviews query, a failed check-in mutation and the review caller reporting it, all within one millisecond. Six Writer “another Mac saved” lines across the four machines indicate conflicts handled by keeping a version. They do not establish lost edits.

**Cause and fix.** Per-request transient retry already exists at `app/apps/server/src/effect/service.ts:97`, with bounded request timeouts at line 150. Independent pollers can still retry together. Introduce per-destination health/backoff for low-priority polls with jitter and one recovery probe, retaining foreground reads and priority renewal/acknowledgement traffic. Distinguish relay, accounts and R2 outages. Replay durable mutations by their existing IDs and surface lock ownership before accepting writes. Summarize a contiguous outage once with counts. Preserve individual failure data for investigation.

**Effort and risk.** 6–12 h, medium. A coarse global circuit could itself cause lease loss. **Verify** by suspending networking for five minutes while two projects sync, renewing a lock near expiry and restoring one destination at a time. Measure outstanding requests, time to recover and duplicate mutations. No overwrite after a different Mac acquires the lock.

### 9. A native abort ends the whole Bun server, and the stack is unsymbolicated — high

**Evidence.** The M4 IPS captured Nov 18 05:18:49.899 +0200 shows `edit`, faulting thread `tokio-rt-worker`, SIGABRT, `abort` and offsets 0x698964 / 0x6986a4 in the embedded .node. That is one native crash, about nine minutes after Writer's ENOSPC. The temporal association is insufficient to blame disk, FFmpeg, a particular Rust panic or memory pressure. Kenobi recorded a separate external/unexplained stop around Nov 17 00:12:36 on 77, with no IPS. JJ and Marc returned zero crash or stop reports.

**Cause and fix.** The blast radius is known because the addon runs in the server process. The abort's cause is unresolved. Release Rust strips symbols (`app/Cargo.toml:62`), and packaging copies the addon from the engine artifact (`.github/workflows/release.yml:249`). Preserve private matching symbols and Mach-O UUID with each engine build, including cache hits. Record current native operation and project on a bounded diagnostic breadcrumb. Resolve this IPS against the **matching** binary before rewriting an engine subsystem. If recurrence implicates one operation, isolate that operation in a restartable worker with a durable job record.

**Effort and risk.** 3–6 h for symbol retention, 8–24 h for targeted isolation once attributed. Low risk for private artifacts, medium-high for moving engine work. **Verify** offline symbolication of a controlled diagnostic stack from the packaged addon. Confirm UUID equality and readable file/line, including a reused engine build. No incident-specific fix can yet be verified.

### 10. Agent sample reviews repeat expensive work after unrelated changes — medium

**Evidence and cause.** M4 logged 57 timed review_motion calls, median 31.0 s, P95 57.9 s. At Nov 18 06:11:28–06:11:54 one model review took 26 s and the tool took 31.0 s. `app/apps/server/src/motion_review.ts:16` serializes every project's sample and model call on one semaphore. Its cache key at line 25 filters overlapping clips but includes the *whole* media array. A strip or cloud metadata update on unrelated footage can therefore invalidate a previously paid review. In the other direction, track-property changes are absent from that key.

**Fix.** Key the sample by the actual render dependencies for that interval, including relevant track visibility/order, media content/version, styles, sound and the review question/model version. Deduplicate identical in-flight keys. Limit capture separately from model concurrency. Preserve provider and memory limits. **Effort and risk.** 4–8 h, medium because a false cache hit hides a defect. **Verify** one model call for the same sample across unrelated backfill, one new call on a material change, and two projects making progress without redundant captures.

### 11. Removal review can skip a second project's result — medium

**Evidence and cause.** `app/apps/server/src/removal_schedule.ts:148` keeps a process-global set of looked-at pieces. Lines 191–204 form keys from treatment, project-local media ID and start time, with no project or result version. After a before/after image is returned, a second project using the same media ID/start, or a replacement strip for the same range, can be skipped. This is a code-path finding. The logs do not count missed inspections.

**Fix.** Scope the inspection record to project and verified result version or digest. Invalidate on replacement/rejection, and expire it when the project leaves memory. **Effort and risk.** 1–2 h, low. **Verify** two projects with the same local IDs followed by a rejected result replaced at the same range. All three results should get their own before/after image. An unchanged result should appear once.

### 12. Remote diagnostics bound returned text after allocating the full response — medium

**Evidence and cause.** `app/apps/server/src/remote_control.ts:151` gives a loopback request a 120 s timeout, then calls `answer.text()` before slicing to OUTPUT_MAX at line 158. A large GET body can therefore occupy server memory and a control slot far beyond the size of the diagnostic answer. This is a bounded-output bug found by inspection. No remote out-of-memory incident was established.

**Fix.** Read at most the answer budget plus one chunk from the body stream, cancel the reader at the limit and mark truncation. Keep the deadline through body consumption. **Effort and risk.** 1–2 h, low. **Verify** using a stubbed slow multi-megabyte response, split UTF-8 characters, and a subsequent small diagnostic request. Check bytes consumed and released resources.
